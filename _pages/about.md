I am an assistant professor (adjunkt) in Business and Data Analytics at the Department of Social Sciences and Business, Roskilde University (RUC), Denmark. I am a management, organization and governance scholar working where network organizations, cross-sectoral partnerships and sustainability transitions meet. My research examines how sustainability transitions are coordinated through complex organizational forms such as collaborative networks, and how management and governance mechanisms structure public-private value delivery and policy implementation.

I am particularly interested in four questions:

- **Metagovernance and design.** How public authorities and other conveners set up, steer and support collaborative networks without controlling them.
- **Firms in public-led partnerships.** Why commercial actors often join co-creation as followers, how they shape value creation and capture to fit their own interests, and how partnerships could accommodate them from the start.
- **Durability and scaling.** Why activity so often stops when a funded project ends, how conflict builds up in collaborative networks, and which organizational capabilities allow co-created innovations to scale.
- **Justice and democracy in collaboration.** Which democratic principles and principles of justice collaborative and participatory governance should be held to, and where in the process they apply.

## Background

From 2022 to 2026 I was a postdoctoral researcher at RUC on Governing Green Transitions (GOGREEN), with Jacob Torfing, Eva Sørensen and Oda Hustad. Much of my current work builds on this project, a comparative study of 42 cases of co-created green transitions and, measured by its shared research protocol and collective data collection, the largest study of its kind in collaborative governance research. I co-authored the project's research protocol and methodology.

I work with mixed methods, combining qualitative case research, qualitative comparative analysis and statistical analysis.

I hold a PhD in Sociology from the University of Copenhagen (2022). My thesis, *The Spatial Division of Labor Between China's Coastal and Inland Regions: Variegated Capitalism with Chinese Characteristics from the Open Door Policy to the New Normal*, examined how states shape spatial organization through infrastructure investment and regional policy. During the PhD I was a visiting scholar at the Sino-Danish Center for Education and Research and the Institute of Geographic Sciences and Natural Resources Research, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing. That work left me with a lasting interest in political economy, comparative historical analysis, port infrastructure and the Belt and Road Initiative.

Before that, I completed a BSc and MSc in International Business and Politics at Copenhagen Business School.
