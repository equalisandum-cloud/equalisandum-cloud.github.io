## Firms in public-led partnerships and ecosystems

My main line of research follows firms, and SMEs in particular, that take part in cross-sectoral partnerships and business ecosystems convened by public authorities. These arrangements are usually organized as time-limited projects. That projectification shapes what firms can gain: commercialization and scaling often stall when funding ends, and firms tend to join as followers of an agenda set elsewhere.

The project asks three things. How do firms position themselves in public-led partnerships? What barriers keep co-created solutions from being scaled or commercialized? And can new tools, including AI-supported aggregation of stakeholder inputs, make collaboration less dependent on individual project cycles? The empirical focus is Region Zealand, where I aim to work with regional business-support and municipal actors.

I am currently collaborating with Joni Askola on how the firms in a circular-economy cross-sectoral partnership accommodate the interests of an SME, building on our work on navigating public sector-led circular ecosystems as a follower.

## GOGREEN: co-created green transitions

GOGREEN is a comparative study of 42 cases of co-created green transitions, carried out by an international team with a shared research protocol and collective data collection and analysis. The project's edited volume, [*Governance of the Local Co-Creation of the Green Transition: A Global Perspective*](https://www.elgaronline.com/edcollbook/book/9781035339587/9781035339587.xml) (Ansell, Sørensen & Torfing, eds., Edward Elgar, 2026), includes the methodology chapter I co-wrote with Oda Hustad and a chapter on managing conflict in co-created sustainable energy projects.

The main project has now concluded, but its large body of data continues to feed many spin-off projects. My own work with the data focuses on metagovernance, network capabilities and the scaling of co-created innovation.

## Justice and democracy in collaborative and participatory governance

My [article in *Public Administration Review*](https://doi.org/10.1111/puar.70179) on ethical pluralism in collaborative and participatory governance brings deliberative, virtue-ethical and agonistic democratic theory together to clarify which democratic principles collaborative governance should be held to, and at what point in the process. I now want to extend this beyond the dominant Western philosophical traditions, drawing on feminist perspectives, East Asian intellectual thought and other traditions to ask how they would reframe justice and participation in collaborative and participatory governance.

## Narratives in collaborative networks

When organizations from different sectors address a shared problem together, they build narratives about what the problem is and why they are working on it. This project examines how those narratives hold a network together, legitimize its work or pull it apart, and treats the articulation of shared narratives as a form of leadership work.

## Co-created job guarantee programs

With Joni Askola, I am developing a theoretical framework for moving from conventional active labor market policies to co-created job guarantee programs, and setting out an agenda for research on them.

## Teaching development

[*The Last Nightcap*](https://equalisandum-cloud.github.io/Nightcap/) is a browser-based murder-mystery game I developed for teaching research design. Students work through a case using induction, abduction and deduction and see how each mode of reasoning constrains what they can conclude.
